03 — RECORDS — CLIENT NAMES WITHHELD, NUMBERS REAL

REC 01 / MANUFACTURING / RAILS · POSTGRESQL · REDIS

From Word documents to an offer-to-order pipeline

CLIENT
An SMB reselling enterprise engineering software (Siemens Teamcenter, NX, and Solid Edge).
PROBLEM
Every salesperson assembled offers by hand in Word and emailed them out, with no standard process, no pricing consistency, and no trail.
BUILT
A web application pulling products, pricing, clients, and contacts live from the company's CMS. Sales composed offers from real data instead of retyping it, tuned pricing within discount parameters, versioned and sent offers, and tracked their status. Accepted offers became orders in the ERP automatically.
OUTCOME
The entire sales team moved onto the system, the offer lifecycle was standardized end to end, and manual-input errors disappeared. I kept evolving it for a year as an independent consultant, then handed it over entirely. The client still runs it without me, which is the point.

REC 02 / PUBLISHING / AIRTABLE · WOOCOMMERCE · AMAZON

Royalties without spreadsheets

CLIENT
A small publisher of music-teaching books for children.
PROBLEM
Sales flowed in from two WooCommerce stores, Amazon, and physical print runs, and were reconciled by hand: reports downloaded manually, stitched together in Excel, royalties calculated author by author.
BUILT
An Airtable-based automation that pulls WooCommerce sales every hour, ingests Amazon and print-shop reports, and computes sales and per-author royalties.
OUTCOME
Manual data-entry errors went to zero, and time spent on sales and royalty reporting dropped by roughly 80%.

REC 03 / EYEWEAR / COMPUTER VISION · PILOT

A computer-vision pilot for eyewear market trends

CLIENT
One of the world's largest eyewear companies.
PROBLEM
No systematic visibility into visual market trends: which shapes, colors, and materials were actually gaining ground.
BUILT
A proof-of-concept that collected eyewear imagery from social networks, brand sites, and specialist e-commerce; trained vision models, each specialized in one product feature, and composed them into a workflow that classified products, produced trend reports, and matched trends against the client's own catalog.
OUTCOME
The pilot proved the approach worked. The client chose not to fund a production phase. An honest result, and a cheap one: that's what pilots are for.
